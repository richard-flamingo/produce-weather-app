About this dashboard — assumptions & logic in plain English

Classification: INTERNAL. A plain-English guide to what the dashboard shows, how the numbers are worked out, and what has been left out.

What it shows

It brings together daily weather observations and short-range forecasts for Flamingo grower sites, and turns them into crop-by-crop weather risk. The weather comes from the Flamingo ‘Weather’ Power BI model. Press Update data in the Cowork sidebar to pull the latest figures.

Crop coverage

Crop risk covers 29 crops across 123 crop × country × grower combinations in 15 countries and 60 grower sites, taken from the Grower crop climate risk by country and grower workbook, which was built from the Crops by Country of Origin sourcing list. That workbook holds 212 combinations across 23 countries; the dashboard shows only the 31 grower/country combinations that have a weather station, so every crop line here can actually raise an alert. Growers without weather — including everything in Guatemala's AVSA, Poland, Rwanda, Turkey, the USA, Zambia, Italy and Mozambique — are in the workbook but not here, and will appear once a site is added to the weather model. Fourteen of the 29 crops shown do not yet have a golden-case yield, so their potential lost yield reads “—”: Baby carrots, Baby leeks, Boston beans, Broccoli (mixed heads), Carrots, Garden peas, Peas (shelled), Petit pois, Rhubarb, Samphire, Savannah beans, Serenade Red (chilli), Stringless beans and Yellow beans.

What has been excluded

Only data from 1 January 2024 onwards is used. Two growers are excluded at the business’s request and do not appear anywhere: Baker Veg Co and Bigot Fleur (France). Duplicate weather rows for the same site and date are removed automatically.

Adverse weather thresholds

Every crop has thresholds beyond which the weather is considered harmful: heat (day too hot), cold (night too cold / frost), heavy rain, and — for crops grown above ground — high wind (damaging gusts). The numbers come from the Grower crop climate risk by country and grower workbook (03/09/2026), one set per crop. Heat uses the onset temperature stated there — Tenderstem broccoli above 30°C, fine and extra fine beans above 30°C, chillies above 32°C. Cold uses the temperature at which the marketable crop or its flowers are damaged: 0°C for beans, peas and chillies, −1°C for asparagus, and 4°C for the broccolis, where a cold check causes buttoning and bolting. Where the workbook describes waterlogging without a millimetre figure, 25 mm in 24 hours is used. Wind is not applied to the crops grown underground — Turmeric, Carrots and Baby carrots.

Peas, mangetout, sugarsnap, petit pois and garden peas have two heat figures, because the workbook gives both. Above 25°C pod set is reduced — rated Mild. Above 30.5°C pollen goes sterile and flowers drop — rated Medium, and Severe beyond 33.5°C. This matters because these crops are grown in hot origins where 25°C is an ordinary day, so a single threshold would show them as permanently at risk.

Severity & potential lost yield

Each adverse event is rated Mild, Medium or Severe depending on how far the weather goes beyond the threshold. The golden-case yield is the tonnes per hectare a crop would give in ideal conditions. Potential lost yield (t/ha) = golden-case yield × a loss percentage set by how sensitive the crop is (sensitivity tiers A, B and C). The same severity-to-loss table is used for heat, cold, rain and wind.

How events are grouped

Observed and forecast breaches are joined together. A run of adverse days that are fewer than 5 days apart is treated as a single event. The Days figure counts only the days the weather was actually over the threshold, not the gaps in between. Where several events fall within 14 days of each other, only the most recent one carries the loss, to avoid double-counting.

Current & upcoming vs past

The Crop risk tab lists events that are ongoing or forecast, plus recent observed events that ended within the last 30 days (shown as “ended”). It only shows crops that are in their growing season right now.

Substitute supply

When a crop is under weather threat, the dashboard suggests other countries growing the same crop that are in season and do not have a significant (Medium or Severe) forecast breach — a starting point for where supply could be topped up.

Forecast accuracy

Each forecast is compared with the actual observation for the same site and date. Same-day (lead 0) forecasts and mean temperature are excluded. For temperature, wind and gust, accuracy = 100% minus the average error as a share of what was observed. Rainfall is scored day by day: a dry day correctly forecast dry counts as 100%, and a wet day is scored on how close the millimetres were. The headline is the average across the reported variables. These figures are a fixed snapshot and are not refreshed by the Update data button.

Actioned risks

Ticking “Actioned” on a Crop risk event moves it to the Actioned risks tab, where you can record what was done. When the dashboard is hosted with the shared “actioned” store, this list is shared across all users and records who actioned each item; if that store isn’t reachable it falls back to your own browser.

Please treat as a guide

The thresholds and loss percentages are informed agronomic estimates, not measured crop responses. Use the dashboard to spot and prioritise risk, and validate important decisions against Flamingo’s own harvest-versus-weather records.
